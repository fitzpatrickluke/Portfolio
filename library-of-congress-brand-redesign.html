Current Identity

What Works

"Casual visitors often fixate on the words 'of Congress,' and are unsure whether they are allowed to use the resources here. Emphasizing the word 'library' as a word mark helps deliver the message that the Library of Congress and its resources are for everyone."

This quote is from the Library's communications officer. They certainly achieved their goal. The current branding feels inviting, makes me want to explore. I love this ideology and idea in the construction of the current brand.

What Doesn't Work

I don't think the library of Congress has bad visual branding. It is unique and contemporary. However it is also bold and loud and does not properly represent the full prestige of a place with such a storied history. The biggest issue presents itself with why on earth do we have "LIBRARY" twice. It is redundant.

Take a step further back and remove all photography and the logo becomes generic. This logo now becomes something that could be used for any library. Not THE Library of Congress. If I were to divorce the words "Library of Congress" from the logo, how would I know that this is for the Library of Congress?

Key Attributes Needed in Redesign

  • Inviting: I should feel like I can use the Library of Congress whenever I want. This was stated as a major reason for the rebrand. The new logo should first and foremost appeal to everyone.
  • America: This is the nation's library, that is essential to convey. If I can't do this through shape can do so through color.
  • Formality: This may contradict inviting but this Library a historic research institute and belongs to the government. It should feel respectable.
  • Library: People should know this logo belongs to a library.
  • Scale: The Library of Congress is one of the largest libraries in the world. The LOC is a castle of books and should be showcased as such.

Initial Explorations

Concept Directions

My main goal with this logo was to have a stylized version of the Library of Congress building. While also having it feel like a bookshelf. I intentionally included room to expand the middle and place images.

I found the previous Library of Congress's branding from 2008 was well done and clever with the mix of a book and american flag. However, I felt it needed to be simplified down and feel less formal. I simplified the design and straightened out the edges.

I played around with combining the L and C to form a book. I sketched out some shapes and mocked them in Illustrator. Eventually I came up with this design. The letterform text was extrapolated from the font Bebas Neue.

I went with the third direction as I kept returning to it as my favorite. For me this logo clicked the most when compared to museum logos. It felt modern and in place among them. I thought it brought a feeling of "this place is cool, I should go there!" Which was my biggest goal in this redesign.

Refinement

Feedback and Revisions

I received multiple critiques that my design was not mature enough to match a government organization. I tried out different serif fonts but none of them seemed to click well or match well with the logo I had.

After much experimentation, I decided to capitalize the organization name. This allowed me to keep the font I had chosen earlier while also bringing a more sophisticated feeling to the design.
