Honolulu 2028 Olympics Brand System

Introduction

In Spring 2026 I took a class in visual identity and branding. Our final project for this course was a group project where we designed an Olympic brand system for a city of our choosing. My group decided to locate our fictional Summer 2028 Olympics in Honolulu, Hawaii. We chose Hawaii due to the vibrant and unqiue art and culture of the islands.

Designing an entire Olympic design system is a huge undertaking. We wanted to focus on quality of quantity. As such for this project my group focused only on designing a primary logo, a few pictograms, choosing a font and color scheme, and dynamic pattern system.

This project was rooted in heavy research as we wanted to be careful not to appropriate any elements of Hawaiin culture. We also designed this system understanding that these islands are extremely limited in space, and the oversaturation of tourism has led to many negative effects for Hawaiian natives. Holding the Olympic games in Hawaii would only be a significant and harmful burden.

Research

History

Honolulu started as a harbor town in the late 1700s, when foreign ships began stopping there to trade for sandalwood and to resupply during whaling voyages. It became the capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom in 1850 and stayed the center of Hawaiian political life through some turbulent times, including the overthrow of Queen Lili'uokalani in 1893 and Hawaii becoming a U.S. territory in 1898. The Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941 put Honolulu at the center of world events and helped push Hawaii toward full statehood, which came in 1959. Today the city is a busy Pacific hub shaped by its Native Hawaiian roots, its immigrant communities, a large military presence, and a tourism industry that touches almost every part of city life.

Visual Traditions

The oldest and most important visual tradition in Hawaii is kapa, a cloth made by pounding the bark of the wauke plant into thin, flexible sheets. Hawaiian women were the primary makers of kapa, and the craft was central to everyday life. Kapa was used for clothing, bedding, and ceremonial purposes.

Designs were applied by stamping or printing geometric patterns onto the surface using carved tools, producing repeating shapes like triangles, lines, and chevrons in natural pigments made from plants and earth.

Defining Characteristics

Honolulu is an extremely popular tourist destination that is defined by its relationship with the natural resources around it. It is known for its beautiful beaches and greenery. Honolulu is extremely crowded, and overrun by tourists in the summer. In contrast with the smaller cities on the rest of Hawaii, Honolulu has a "big city" energy that is comparable to the feeling of Los Angeles.

Key Attributes Needed in Design

  • Authentic: Hawaii has a long history of colonization, and we seek to highlight the native artistic and cultural roots of the islands.
  • Naturalistic: Hawaii is famous for its pristine natural resources, and natural beauty is a key pillar of Hawaiian culture. Our brand identity reflects the native flora.
  • Cosmopolitan: The city of Honolulu has a vibrant, contemporary character. We want the brand to look modern.
  • Energetic: Let's not forget this is for the Olympics. The design should feel active, dynamic, and exciting.

Past Olympic Identity Inspirations

We used the Barcelona pictograms as inspiration for ours, but wanted to focus on angular geometric forms instead of loose and organic. Barcelona's brand identity reads as very rustic, while we wanted to communicate a sleek and modern feeling.

We believe logo also plays with radial symmetry, and evoke the form of the yellow hibiscus flower. A notable Hawaiian flower. During design explorestion we frequently returned to the Salt Lake City logo as inspiration for creating a more abstract and stylistic version of an object.

The Mexico City logo uses repeating patterns as a core part of the visual identity, which is something we sought to do with indigenous Kapa patterns.

Initial Explorations

Playing with patterns a lot. How can we frame the logo within Kapa patterns? What if we tried a flower? How can we frame the flower? Hawaii is the rainbow capitol, can we incorporate that somehow? Emphasis on thick, bold, letters and patterns.

Flower Development

We were very inspired by our logos that went in a flower direction. Using the Hibiscus as a base we toyed around with simplifying the flower and utilizing patterns within it.

We continued to simplify this shape and make it more and more angular until we were satisfied with the final direction.

Text and Pattern Development

Inspiration

We knew we need to developed a pattern system for our system. We drew inspiration directly from existing Polynesion patterns to ensure authenticity.

Font and Framing

We played around with many different fonts to see what would fit best within these complex patterns. We quickly discovered we enojyed the look og these patterns framoing the city, year, and olympic logo.

These patterns started out much smaller and detailed and when through a continous proces of enlargement and simplification to read better from a distance.

Final Patterns

To ensure consistancy across the logo and system as a whole we finalized a concrete set of 7 patterns. These were designed to be the same height to ensure ease of mix and matching the patterns.

Refinement

The flower and pattern system were developed concurrently. However, once we put them together we worked to ensure angles, dimensions, and spacing were consistant across the design.

Pictograms

Our pictogram designes utilized similar shape language as the rest of design: Bold and triangular. While designing these we focused on emphasizing movement and dynanasism of the sports poses.
