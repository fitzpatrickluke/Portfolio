Denver Zoo Website Redesign

Introduction

During my course in UX design, I was tasked with redesigning the ticket purchasing process for the Denver Zoo website. This process led me through the fundamental steps of UX design. In the end, I was able to turn the negative experience of purchasing a ticket on the Denver Zoo website into a seamless and enjoyable activity. I was the sole designer and researcher for this project.

Challenges

My biggest challenge and limitation throughout this entire process was time. Being a full-time student, I had to diligently split my time between all my classes. In an ideal situation, I would have had more time to sketch and develop my wireframes and conduct more user testing on which to iterate my prototype. This limitation led my to making some last minute changes and additions to my design that, with more time, could have been more thought out and effective.

The biggest example of this is my initial font choice appearing too large. In a short time frame, I had to decrease almost all font sizes. This in turn, effected all other design elements that I had to rapidly edit. This challenge taught me a valuable lesson on quickly pivoting a design.

Research

User Testing

To begin redesigning the Denver Zoo website, I conducted user testing to gain a greater understanding of the current ticket purchasing process. I conducted four user interviews. The testing process had each tester click through the existing Denver Zoo website while commenting on their thoughts and issues with each page. Users would also state what page they would expect to see next.

I received unanimous negative feedback on the Denver Zoo's current experience. By far the greatest pain point experienced by all users was the time that it took to purchase a ticket. Users felt like they had to go through too many pages to complete this ideally simple task. All users were annoyed by the time had reached the checkout page.

Competitive Analysis

In my competitive analysis, I simulated the process of purchasing a day ticketed various zoo and aquarium websites. I analyzed locations within Colorado and the western U.S. region.

Questions Uncovered During Research

How might we allow users to easily return to previous pages to change parts of their order?

How might we inform users about additional amenities that the zoo offers for purchase?

How might we easily and quickly invite users to purchase tickets from the home page?

Problem Statement

Families need a way to easily and efficiently purchase tickets online while still being able to add different amenities offered by the Denver zoo to make their trip as pleasant as possible.

Design

User Flow

I designed a user flow diagram for the golden path a user would take when purchasing a ticket from the Denver Zoo website. This user flow outlined the ideal path based upon my research and informed my prototype going forward.

Low Fidelity Wireframes

Mid Fidelity Wireframes

Once I had a solid idea of the general layout of my prototype, I developed mid fidelity wireframes in Figma. This step had me finalize the layout of my current idea. Without worrying about color and images, I was able to focus on elements such as spacing, font size, and all final text. I made an effort to make my elements into components to ease the creation of my high fidelity wireframe.

I also changed the order of pages between my low and high fidelity wireframes, swapping the order of the Tickets and Dates page.

High Fidelity Wireframes

Using both my mid fidelity wireframes as a base and the feedback I received from other students and my professor, I moved on to create a high fidelity wireframe. I focused on making elements cohesive in color and adding final photography. I also updated any typos or spacing issues from my mid fidelity wireframe.

Evaluation

Instructor Feedback

After I completed my high fidelity wireframe, I received feedback from my instructor that I put to use to further improve my prototype.

Key feedback included certain elements not passing accessibility checks, buttons not looking like they could be clicked, and inconsistency of font sizes and element spacing across pages.

User Testing

To further improve my prototype, I conducted user testing on two users. Meeting with my users in-person, I had them click through my high fidelity prototype while they commented their overall thoughts for each page. This included their opinion of what they found clear or confusing, as well as what they expected the next page to be.

Overall, I received positive feedback from my users. The general flow of buying a ticket matched my users expectations and was easy for them to complete.

However, both users expressed similar confusion with aspects of the design. First was the use of a weekly calendar over a monthly calendar. Both users expressed that a monthly calendar would be easier to follow and make planning trips easier.

They also both wanted more information on why one would want to login. They questioned what benefits an account would provide.

One of my user testers also expressed confusion regarding what buttons were clickable. Specifically, the radio buttons on the Tickets page and signing up for the newsletter in the footer. This user also expressed that it would be helpful to have suggested donation amounts in the Checkout page.

My other user thought that it would make sense for the food packages to have their own section in the Extras page. This user also expressed that they thought the checkout page had too much empty space on the right.

Updates

My feedback was very helpful and I utilized it to create beneficial updates to my prototype. First, I listened to feedback from my professor and took action to update all non accessible color pairings in my prototype.

Per my user's feedback, I changed the weekly calendar into a monthly calendar and added a description next to the "Log in" button. I also added recommended donation amounts in the donation description.

Reflection

Future Plans

If I were to continue with this redesign and prototype, I would like to conduct user more interviews. While I received positive feedback from my prototype, this current prototype is far from perfect. I feel this current design lacks a unique spark that can take it from good to great.

Conclusion

This entire process, from research to evaluation, was a valuable learning experience. My biggest takeaway from all of this was becoming comfortable with Figma as well as the practice of implementing user feedback into a design. I also gained knowledge regarding the main purposes of the three different types of wireframes and the typical UX and front end design process for software.
