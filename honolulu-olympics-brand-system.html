Honolulu 2028 Olympics Brand System

Introduction

In Spring 2026 I took a class in visual identity and branding. Our final project for this course was a group project where we designed an Olympic brand system for a city of our choosing. My group decided to locate our fictional Olympics in Honolulu, Hawaii. We chose Hawaii due to the vibrant and unqiue art and culture of the islands.

This project was rooted in heavy research as we wanted to be careful not to appropriate any elements of Hawaiin culture. We also designed this system understanding that these islands are extremely limited in space, and the oversaturation of tourism has led to many negative effects for Hawaiian natives. Holding the Olympic games in Hawaii would only be a significant and harmful burden.

Research

History

Honolulu started as a harbor town in the late 1700s, when foreign ships began stopping there to trade for sandalwood and to resupply during whaling voyages. It became the capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom in 1850 and stayed the center of Hawaiian political life through some turbulent times, including the overthrow of Queen Lili'uokalani in 1893 and Hawaii becoming a U.S. territory in 1898. The Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor in 1941 put Honolulu at the center of world events and helped push Hawaii toward full statehood, which came in 1959. Today the city is a busy Pacific hub shaped by its Native Hawaiian roots, its immigrant communities, a large military presence, and a tourism industry that touches almost every part of city life.

Visual Traditions

The oldest and most important visual tradition in Hawaii is kapa, a cloth made by pounding the bark of the wauke plant into thin, flexible sheets. Hawaiian women were the primary makers of kapa, and the craft was central to everyday life, kapa was used for clothing, bedding, and ceremonial purposes. The designs were applied by stamping or printing geometric patterns onto the surface using carved tools, producing repeating shapes like triangles, lines, and chevrons in natural pigments made from plants and earth.

Defining Characteristics

Honolulu is an extremely popular tourist destination that is defined by its relationship with the natural resources around it. It is known for its beautiful beaches and greenery. Honolulu is extremely crowded, and overrun by tourists in the summer. In contrast with the smaller cities on the rest of Hawaii, Honolulu has a “big city” energy that is comparable to the feeling of Los Angeles.

Past Olympic Identity Inspirations

We used the Barcelona pictograms as inspiration for ours, but focused on the angular geometric forms instead of loose and organic. Barcelona's brand identity reads as very rustic, while we were trying to communicate a more sleek and modern feeling.

Our logo also plays with radial symmetry, and we're evoking the form of the yellow hibiscus flower. Our initial drafts were too literal and representational, and we used the Salt Lake City logo as inspiration for creating a more abstract and stylistic version of an object while maintaining recognizability.

The Mexico City logo uses repeating patterns as a core part of the visual identity, which is something we sought to do with indigenous Kapa patterns. We used the Mexico City brand identity as inspiration for our generative element.

Key Attributes

  • Authentic: Hawaii has a long history of colonization, and we seek to highlight the native artistic and cultural roots of the islands.
  • Naturalistic: Hawaii is famous for its pristine natural resources, and natural beauty is a key pillar of Hawaiian culture. Our brand identity reflects the native flora.
  • Cosmopolitan: The city of Honolulu has a vibrant, contemporary character. We want the brand to look modern.
