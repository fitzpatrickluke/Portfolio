Denver Zoo Website Redesign

Introduction

During Spring 2024, I took a course in user experience design. My focus during the semester was redesigning the website for Second & Charles. Second & Charles is a retail store that sells and buys new and used books, music, video games, and more. Their current online experience was inconsistent, inaccessible, and frustrating to navigate.

My goal was to develop a greater user experience while keeping the fun and bright visual style of the Second and Charles brand. This project exposed me of stages of the design process for both desktop and mobile experiences. I was the sole designer and researcher for this project.

Challenges

My main challenge throughout this process was designing an experience that would be easy to use and pleasing to the eye while also keeping the bright and colorful visual identity of Second & Charles.

Initially, I found that my attempt to clarify content stripped the experience of any fun. Yet, I pushed through and completed a prototype that still captures the brand's unique essence while maintaining accessibility.

Research

Evaluation Audit

It was vital to understand the current user experience of the Second & Charles website, so I conducted an evaluation audit of the current experience. During the audit, I analyzed 50 different features and design conventions. I discovered key takeaways such as inconsistency in font style and color, difficulty in locating clickable components, and failure to pass accessibility standards. Many other smaller issues existed, culminating in an overall negative experience.

Design

Userflow

I planned out eight pages that I would redesign for both desktop and mobile. The process I settled on involved adding a bestselling book to the cart and creating an account.

The actions needed to perform these tasks were few yet convoluted due to the poor visual design and clutter of the site.

Low Fidelity Wireframes

A major flaw with the current experience was the cluttered and claustrophobic layout. I removed, renamed, and moved around existing elements in my sketches. I looked at competing book selling websites to help me generate additional ideas and possibilities for page layouts.

Mid Fidelity Wireframes

In Figma, I designed mid fidelity wireframes based on my sketches. This process was relatively straightforward to complete. I added all text that would appear on my final prototype. My sketches only had placeholder text.

However, I did make some missteps at this point, such as choosing a font that did not match the design aesthetic I wanted to achieve. I also forgot to include the names of all the shopping items. Both of these errors led to some unnecessary work near the end of the design process that could have been avoided.

High Fidelity Wireframes

Next, I moved on to creating a high fidelity design in Figma, building upon my mid fidelity design. I added color, images, and finalized all text. I restructured and removed elements that did not translate well with the styling I used.

This step in the design process was where I encountered my main challenge. I wanted to keep the Second & Charles brand identity while also designing a strong user experience. I found my design lacked all the color and fun descriptions that made Second & Charles unique shopping experience.

While I was able to finalize text and spacing, this took an unexpected amount of time. This time delay resulted in my running out of time to complete shopping banners for the Home and Books page.

Evaluation

User Testing

Implementing user feedback was instrumental for this project's success. I conducted user testing with three students on my high fidelity prototype. I met with my testers in person and had them click through both the desktop and mobile prototypes while I took notes and asked a set series of questions for each page. I had fifteen pages of notes by the end of the tests.

My users easily completed all designated tasks. I was glad to hear this! Among my users, the two biggest complaints were on the Books page and the Bestsellers page.

On the Books page, multiple users expressed interest in wanting the categories section at the top. They stated that if they are looking for a specific book or book genre, they would prefer not to have to scroll to the bottom of a page. On the Bestsellers page, there was confusion if the Add to cart button was referencing the item below or above.

Other recurring complaints included wanting the cart icon to have an indicator for when an item gets added and numbers being too small on mobile. Users also expressed not wanting to input their phone number and birthday when creating an account. Lastly, users wanted the header visible on all pages for mobile.

Instructor Feedback

The main feedback I received was certain items needed to be spaced out a bit more, certain buttons were primary when they should be secondary, and text inputs were too small. These problems appeared in both the desktop and mobile experience.

My professor and I also discussed accessibility issues that I encountered while designing with the Second & Charles brand colors. Their use of white text on a light orange background did not follow AAA WCAG accessibility guidelines. We decided to switch the orange with the secondary color of dark teal.

Updates

I found implementing all the feedback I had received made the prototype feel more complete and enabled a stronger user experience.

During this step I also completed all the shopping banners, using inspiration from the current experience and other online shopping websites. I focused heavily on updating colors and images to create a more pleasing yet fun visual appeal to the website. These updates added much more personality into my design.

Reflection

Future Plans

There are numerous ways to enhance this prototype. Due to time limitations as a student and limiting myself to eight screens, my user flow was missing some pages that would have enhanced the experience.

Furthermore, I stuck heavily to the existing 2nd & Charles brand system. Yet I feel with more time, it would have been a fun and interesting challenge myself to see how I could update the brand system to fix the many user experience issues it contained.

Conclusion

Overall, I am pleased with the knowledge I gained through this entire process. My biggest takeaway was how to design for a mobile experience. I challenged myself to work within the Second & Charles overall brand design system. Their current system was less than ideal to work with, and challenged me to creatively problem solve. This challenging opportunity provided me with valuable experience for the overall UX design process.
